 6.  THE FIVE RULES THAT CHANGE WHAT YOU DO
================================================================================
 These are from the 2026 MWCCDC team pack. They are not general advice.

 1. NO SOURCE-IP FILTERING ON PUBLIC SERVICES.  (Systems rule 9)
    "Teams must maintain 'public' services as generally available from all source
    IP addresses. Attempts to restrict or filter by IP source address may
    adversely affect scoring directly, and may also incur a penalty when
    detected. Teams are prohibited from configuring any sort of knife edge
    access, attempting to allow only scoring engine traffic."
    -> You MAY block a specific IP that is actively attacking you. You may NOT
       allow-list the scoring engine. The kit refuses to do this now.

 2. NO SNAPSHOTS, NO SCRUBS, NO REVERTS.  (Systems rule 10)
    "Teams will not have the ability to scrub or revert to snapshot a VM during
    the invitational. Tech Support will not support any snapshot/scrub."
    -> Every change is permanent. Back up before you touch anything. This is why
       the firewall auto-reverts and sshd restores itself: those are your only
       undo. You can request a whole new lab reservation, but service scores up
       to that point are retained -- it is not a free reset.

 3. MAINTAIN ICMP.  (Competition Play rule 12)
    "Blue Team members should maintain ICMP on all competition devices and
    systems, including the router, unless directed otherwise."
    -> Ping is a scored service. The kit always allows echo-request. Do not
       block it anywhere, including on the VyOS router and the Palo Alto.

 4. YOU MAY NOT CHANGE USERNAMES.  (Competition Play rule 11)
    "Blue Team members may not change usernames within their respective
    environment... may change passwords for administrator and user level
    accounts."
    -> Rename nothing. Passwords only. Deleting or disabling an account is a
       grey area -- if in doubt, disable rather than delete, and note it in an
       incident report.

 5. NO SYSTEM NAME OR IP CHANGES.  (Systems rules 6 and 7)
    "Teams are not permitted to alter the system names or IP address of their
    assigned systems unless directed by an inject; this may affect the results
    of the scoring mechanism." Services must stay on their assigned public IP.
    -> No hostname changes, no re-addressing, no moving a service to another VM.

 8.  WHAT TO CHECK ALL DAY (THE LOOP)
================================================================================
Every 15 minutes, on each box you own. Two minutes each.

  [ ] Quotient (quotient.ccdc.events). Is anything red? That is the only
      opinion that counts.
  [ ] sudo bash services.sh check          are my scored services up?
  [ ] sudo bash baseline.sh diff           what changed since I hardened?
  [ ] tail -20 /root/ccdc-logs/services.log   did anything restart itself?
  [ ] sudo bash splunk.sh check            is this box still forwarding logs?

  Every 30-45 minutes, or any time something feels wrong:
  [ ] sudo bash persistence.sh             full hunt
  [ ] sudo bash users.sh audit             new accounts? new SSH keys?
  [ ] ss -tulpn                            new listening ports?
  [ ] ps -ef | grep -E '/tmp|/dev/shm'     anything running from a temp dir?
  [ ] last -20                             unexpected logins?
  [ ] Splunk: index=* "Accepted password"  logins you did not make?
  [ ] On the Splunk box: sudo bash splunk.sh indexer   is every box still sending?

  THE FIVE QUESTIONS THAT CATCH MOST ATTACKS
    1. Is there a UID 0 account that is not root?
            awk -F: '$3==0{print $1}' /etc/passwd
    2. Did an SSH key appear that I did not add?
            find / -name authorized_keys -newer /etc/hostname 2>/dev/null
    3. Is something listening that was not an hour ago?     ss -tulpn
    4. Is a process running out of /tmp, /var/tmp or /dev/shm?
            ps -ef | grep -E '/tmp|/dev/shm|/var/tmp' | grep -v grep
    5. Did a system binary change?     sudo bash pkgcheck.sh verify
    6. Is this box still forwarding to MY indexer, not someone else's?
            sudo bash splunk.sh check

  WHEN YOU FIND SOMETHING -- IN THIS ORDER
    1. WRITE IT DOWN FIRST. What, where, timestamp, source IP. This is the
       incident report, and incident response is 20% of your score. You will
       not remember the details later.
    2. Find how they got in. A webshell means the web app is the hole. A new
       SSH key means credentials leaked. Removing the artifact without closing
       the door means they are back in five minutes.
    3. Then remove it. Re-check in 20 minutes. If it returns, you missed the
       persistence, not the payload.

 9.  SPLUNK -- EVERY BOX FEEDS IT
================================================================================
Splunk is not just the Debian box's problem. Every machine on the network must
forward its logs to the indexer at 172.20.240.20:9997. Two scored checks and a
fifth of your total score depend on that working.

 WHY IT IS A SCORING ISSUE, NOT JUST A LOGGING ONE
   * "Splunk Query" authenticates and searches for SPECIFIC CONTENT. If the
     forwarders on your other boxes stop sending, that search can come back
     empty -- so a dead forwarder on Rocky can fail the SPLUNK box's check.
   * "Splunk Management" (8089) is its own scored check.
   * Incident response is 20% of the score and needs source IPs and timelines.
     No logs, no report, no points.

 ON EVERY BOX -- do this during the first hardening pass
     sudo bash splunk.sh check          READ ONLY. Changes nothing.
   It reports: is the forwarder installed, is the service running, WHERE is it
   sending, is the connection actually established, and what is being collected.
   It needs no Splunk password -- it reads config files, systemctl and ss.

     sudo bash splunk.sh fix            repair forwarding (asks before each change)
     sudo bash splunk.sh watch          add Splunk to the services.sh watchdog

 ON THE SPLUNK BOX (172.20.240.20)
     sudo bash splunk.sh indexer        who is actually connected and sending?
   If a box is missing from that list, it is not forwarding. Go run 'check' on it.
   Then confirm inside Splunk itself:
       | metadata type=hosts | eval last=strftime(lastTime,"%H:%M:%S")
         | table host last totalCount
   Every box you own should appear, with a recent timestamp.

 *** CHECK BOOT-START FIRST (found on the practice VM, 13 Nov) ***
   Splunk can be running happily with NO systemd unit and NO init script. It looks
   fine until something reboots the box -- then it stays down, Splunk Query and
   Splunk Management both fail, and every forwarder has nowhere to send logs.
   The watchdog cannot restart it either, because services.sh uses systemctl.
       sudo bash splunk.sh check          look at the "managed by:" line
       sudo bash splunk.sh fix            offers to register it with systemd
   By hand (Splunk REFUSES to register boot-start while running, so stop first --
   about 1-2 min of downtime, cheaper than losing it to a reboot):
       ps -o user= -p $(pgrep -x splunkd | head -1)    # run it back as THAT user
       sudo /opt/splunk/bin/splunk stop
       sudo /opt/splunk/bin/splunk enable boot-start -systemd-managed 1 -user <thatuser>
       sudo systemctl daemon-reload
       sudo systemctl enable --now Splunkd
       systemctl is-active Splunkd
       sudo bash splunk.sh watch
   If the unit never appears, start Splunk again immediately so you are not down:
       sudo /opt/splunk/bin/splunk start

 *** SPLUNK 10 COLD-START RACE (seen on the practice box, 13 Nov) ***
   On a cold start -- including after a reboot -- splunkd can come up while the web
   tier stays DOWN. Port 8089 and 9997 look fine, but 8000 never opens, and 8000 is
   the SCORED Splunk Query check. The splunkd log shows:
       WebuiStartup ... Connection refused ... 127.0.0.1:8065
   8065 is Splunk 10's Python web backend; the UI starts before it is ready.
   THE FIX IS SIMPLY A SECOND RESTART:
       sudo systemctl restart Splunkd      (then give it ~2 minutes)
       ss -tlnp | grep -E ':8000|:8065'    both should be listening
   This is why the watchdog matters here: with Splunk registered as a systemd unit,
   services.sh sees 8000 missing and restarts Splunkd, which resolves the race by
   itself. Make sure it is watching:
       sudo bash splunk.sh watch
   ALWAYS CHECK 8000 AFTER ANY REBOOT OR RESTART. Do not trust "active" alone --
   systemd reports the unit, not whether the scored port is actually open.

 *** THE ATTACK TO WATCH FOR ***
   Red team edits outputs.conf and re-points your forwarder at THEIR box. That
   does two things at once: they get a copy of your logs, and you go blind. It
   is quiet and easy to miss. 'splunk.sh check' flags any forward target that is
   not your indexer, and the guided run surfaces it in the RED FLAGS block.
   Config lives at:
       /opt/splunkforwarder/etc/system/local/outputs.conf     (forwarders)
       /opt/splunk/etc/system/local/outputs.conf              (indexer)
   Also watch inputs.conf -- deleting the monitors leaves the forwarder running
   while collecting nothing, which looks healthy and is not.

 PORTS -- DO NOT PRUNE THESE
   On the Splunk box:  9997 (forwarders in), 8000 (query check), 8089 (SCORED
                       management check), possibly 514/udp (syslog).
   On every other box: leave OUTBOUND open. The kit never blocks outbound, but
                       if you hand-write rules, remember forwarders dial OUT.

 IF A FORWARDER WILL NOT START
     systemctl status SplunkForwarder    (unit may be Splunkd or splunk)
     /opt/splunkforwarder/bin/splunk status
     tail -40 /opt/splunkforwarder/var/log/splunk/splunkd.log
   Restarting Splunk is slow -- give it a couple of minutes before deciding it
   failed.

 SEARCHES WORTH KNOWING (for incident reports)
     index=* "Failed password"                    SSH brute force
     index=* "Accepted password"                  logins -- any you did not make?
     index=* sudo                                 privilege escalation
     index=* sourcetype=linux_secure | stats count by host
     | metadata type=hosts                        who is reporting in at all

 10.  INCIDENT REPORTS -- 20% OF THE SCORE
================================================================================
This is the most under-used points pool in CCDC, and this year it is a full
fifth of the total. The pack says reports are scored on "clarity, thoroughness
and accuracy" and must contain:

  [ ] A description of what occurred
  [ ] Timelines of activity
  [ ] Source AND destination IP addresses
  [ ] Any passwords cracked
  [ ] A discussion of what was affected
  [ ] A remediation plan

  Submit them through the White Team. They can generally be filed as needed
  throughout the competition.

  WARNING from the pack: the White Team "may also, at their discretion, assess
  NEGATIVE scores for frivolous, unnecessary, or excessive communication."
  So do not file a report for every failed SSH login. File real incidents,
  written properly.

  Keep a running scratch file from minute one:
      echo "$(date '+%H:%M') found X on box Y from IP Z" >> ~/incidents.txt
  Splunk (172.20.240.20) is where you get IPs and timelines. Use it.

 11.  INJECTS -- 40% OF THE SCORE
================================================================================
  [ ] One person owns injects FULL TIME from minute zero. Not "whoever is free."
  [ ] Portal: https://injects.ccdc.events   (log in FIRST, before anything else)
  [ ] Answer the "Welcome" inject immediately -- it signals you are ready.
  [ ] *** RESPONSES MUST BE AN ATTACHED PDF FILE. *** Any exception will be
      stated in the inject itself. Judges will reject responses that do not
      conform to the stated requirements.
  [ ] NISE runs on CENTRAL time. Do not submit in the last minute of validity --
      the pack explicitly warns that last-minute submissions get rejected.
  [ ] While an inject is still open, both you and the judges can mark a
      submission invalid so you can resubmit. There is a resubmission limit.
  [ ] Nextcloud is provided for drafting responses and has an online office
      suite. Use it rather than fighting with a VM text editor.
  [ ] Read the inject twice before writing, once more before submitting.
      Answer exactly what is asked -- rubrics reward that.

 12.  GENERAL ADVICE
================================================================================
SCORING BEATS SECURITY.
  A hardened box that fails its check scores zero. A slightly soft box that
  answers every check scores full marks. When in doubt, keep the service up.

THERE IS NO UNDO THIS YEAR.
  No snapshots, no scrubs, no reverts. Back up before you touch anything, and
  copy backups off the box. The firewall's 5-minute auto-revert and sshd's
  self-restore are the only safety nets you have. Use the dry run.

A LISTENING PORT IS NOT A WORKING SERVICE.
  HTTP/HTTPS checks compare page CONTENT. Mail checks send and receive real
  mail. Splunk checks authenticate and run a query. After any change, test the
  service the way the scoring engine would, not just 'systemctl status'.

MAIL DEPENDS ON THE WINDOWS TEAM.
  POP3/IMAP authenticate against Active Directory usernames. Coordinate before
  anyone rotates AD passwords, or mail scoring dies for reasons the Linux owner
  cannot see.

SPLUNK IS A WHOLE-NETWORK SERVICE, NOT ONE BOX.
  Every machine forwards to 172.20.240.20:9997. Run 'splunk.sh check' on every
  box during hardening and again in the loop. A dead forwarder on Rocky can fail
  the SPLUNK box's scored query check, and it costs you the evidence behind the
  20% incident-response score. Port 9997 inbound on the Splunk box, outbound
  open everywhere else.

KEEP A SECOND SESSION OPEN. ALWAYS.
  Before touching sshd or the firewall, open a second session and leave it idle.
  Your NETLAB+ console is the backstop -- know how to reach it.

CHANGE ONE THING AT A TIME, THEN TEST.
  Three changes at once and you cannot tell which broke scoring.

WRITE IT ON PAPER.
  Passwords, what you changed, what you found and when. Printed materials are
  permitted. The pack also warns competition officials are not responsible for
  the security of credentials you put on the competition network.

ONE OWNER PER BOX. Two people editing one config is self-inflicted downtime.

BUDGET FOR FOUR HOURS, NOT EIGHT.
  Rough split: 40% uptime and monitoring, 40% injects, 20% hunting and reports.
  You will not finish hardening everything. Protect what is scored first.

 13.  WHEN SOMETHING BREAKS
================================================================================
 LOCKED OUT BY THE FIREWALL
     Wait 5 minutes -- it reverts itself.
     From the NETLAB+ console:   sudo sh /root/UNDO-FIREWALL.sh
     Or:                         sudo bash firewall.sh undo

 SSH BROKE AFTER HARDENING
     The kit restores sshd_config automatically if the config fails its test.
     Still locked out? NETLAB+ console, then:
         ls /etc/ssh/sshd_config.bak.*
         cp /etc/ssh/sshd_config.bak.<newest> /etc/ssh/sshd_config
         systemctl restart sshd   (or ssh)

 A SERVICE WILL NOT START
     systemctl status <name> ; journalctl -u <name> -n 50
     On Rocky, check SELinux FIRST:
         getenforce ; ausearch -m avc -ts recent ; restorecon -Rv <path>
     Never 'setenforce 0' to make it go away.

 WEB PAGE LOADS BUT SCORES ZERO
     The check compares content. Compare the live page against the docroot
     backup at /opt/html.BAK.*  -- red team may have altered the page.

 MAIL AUTH FAILING
     Ask the Windows team what changed in AD. POP3/IMAP use AD usernames.

 SPLUNK DOWN
     systemctl status Splunkd ; /opt/splunk/bin/splunk status
     /opt/splunk/bin/splunk restart      (slow, give it 2 minutes)

 SERVICE KEEPS DYING AFTER RESTART
     That is someone killing it, not a crash:
         sudo bash persistence.sh ; systemctl list-timers --all
         crontab -l ; cat /etc/cron.d/*

 LOST THE NEW PASSWORDS
     Shredded on purpose. Use the NETLAB+ console and set them again with
     plan-b/2-pw.sh, then resubmit via Quotient PCR.

 VM COMPLETELY LOCKED UP
     You can power cycle it from the NETLAB+ VE console. You CANNOT scrub or
     revert it. A new lab reservation is possible but scores up to that point
     are retained -- it is not a clean restart.

 COMMON MISTAKES
     "Permission denied"  -> you forgot sudo
     "No such file"       -> wrong directory; cd into ccdc/ first
     Script will not run  -> use  bash script.sh  or  sh script.sh

 14.  THINGS THAT COST POINTS
================================================================================
  * Filtering a public service by source IP, or allow-listing the scoring
    engine. Rule 9: may incur a penalty when detected, and knife-edge access is
    prohibited outright.
  * Blocking ICMP anywhere. Ping is scored and rule 12 says maintain it.
  * Changing a username. Rule 11 forbids it. Passwords only.
  * Changing a system name or IP, or moving a service to another VM.
  * Changing a password and not submitting it via Quotient PCR.
  * Breaking Splunk forwarding, or missing that red team re-pointed a forwarder
    at their own box. You lose the logs AND hand them a copy.
  * Running the kit on the VyOS router or the Palo Alto.
  * Any offensive activity against another team, White Team or Red Team --
    IMMEDIATE DISQUALIFICATION. Scanning your own systems is fine. Nothing else.
  * Using a private external staging area for tools. Must be freely available
    to all teams.
  * Installing non-free software. Free or open source only, no trials.
  * Filing frivolous or excessive incident reports -- can score NEGATIVE.
  * Identifying your school to the White Team. Use your team NUMBER.
